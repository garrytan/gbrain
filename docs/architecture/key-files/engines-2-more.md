- `src/core/rust-engine/owner-ipc/` — the owner protocol of the Rust engine (ENGINES.md "The Rust engine shell"; design in `docs/designs/RUST_ENGINE.md`). One process owns a brain: `election.ts` `electOwner` takes the kernel lock `<database_path>.gbrain-owner.lock`, runs the engine's `onLock` step (the Rust engine opens its store there, so no call reaches the owner without it and a client that connects meanwhile finds no socket yet), makes the store directory mode 0700 (created `0o700` and `chmod`ed, whatever the socket path length) and the owner serves its engine on `<database_path>/.gbrain-owner.sock` (`server.ts`: 32 calls at once, the rest wait in a FIFO lane per connection and a free permit goes to the lanes in turn, so one client that floods the owner does not starve another, at most 1,024 calls in all, at most 256 MiB of call frames held at once (queued and running), at most 256 connections, each over a bound refused `owner_busy` with reason `queue_full`, `inflight_bytes` or `connections`; a refused connection gets one `refused` frame and is half-closed, a refused peer that never closes its side is cut off after 1 second (`OWNER_IPC_REFUSED_DRAIN_MS`), and past 64 refusals in flight the next is dropped with no frame; a cancel frame ends a call, `deadline_at` and `read_deadline_at` bound it; where the kernel gives a peer's uid (`src/core/peer-cred.ts`) a connection from another OS user gets a `refused` frame `owner_ipc_peer_refused` reason `peer_uid` and nothing runs); every other process becomes a client (`client.ts` `installOwnerClient`, never opens the store, capabilities mirror the owner's hello; before it connects it refuses a socket file another user owns, `socket_owner`, or one with group or other permission bits outside a private 0700 directory of this user, `socket_mode`, and after it connects an owner of another uid, `peer_uid`; for a remote caller, `callerIsRemote`: a handler call marked by `src/core/remote-caller.ts` (an MCP request, a subagent brain tool or a SkillOpt tool), a job whose submission authority is not the application, or a remote verified writer, it sends only the reads of `routes.ts` `OWNER_IPC_REMOTE_READS` and refuses any other member `engine_capability_missing` with nothing sent) or, after 5 seconds, throws `owner_unavailable` with reason `not_sent`. `routes.ts` classifies each member: `local` (`kind`, `capabilities`, `connect`, `disconnect`, `reconnect`, `registerBeforeDisconnect`), `refuse` (`transaction`, `transactionDirect`, `withReservedConnection`, `holdSchemaMigrationLock`, `keywordBoundedRead`, `worktreeClaimStore`, `writerAdminStore`; against a Rust owner all but `holdSchemaMigrationLock` first meet the capability check below and refuse with the owner's own reason, `transactions` or `raw_sql`; `holdSchemaMigrationLock`, the two store getters (their calls run inside a transaction) and a call whose arguments hold a function other than a search metadata hook refuse `engine_capability_missing` with `via=owner_ipc`) or `forward` (everything else; a member the owner's hello lacks, and the store does not serve, refuses at the client as the owner's own engine does). `whole-op.ts` and `owner-ops.ts` can forward `importFromContent`, `importFromFile`, `importImageFile` and `importManagedFiles` as one request that runs, transaction included, in the owner (paths are made absolute, and a remote caller or a function option refuses). A whole operation is sent only to an owner whose hello reports `persistence_journal`; a client of an owner without it refuses `engine_capability_missing` (reason `persistence_journal`) and sends nothing. `forwardOwnerOperation` sends a registry page write (`OWNER_FORWARDED_OPERATIONS`, `put_page`) the same way: the owner runs the operation's own handler, so the caller gets the receipt or error it would in process, and notices and log lines come back with the result. `protocol.ts` and `codec.ts` frame the calls (an owner reply over 128 MiB ends `response_too_large`; a call's `access` field carries the caller's access record, which the owner runs it under; `deadline_at` ends a queued call `timeout` reason `not_started` and a running one `outcome_unknown`; `timer.ts` `setDeadlineTimer` re-arms past the 2^31-1 ms setTimeout limit on both sides, so a far deadline waits instead of firing at once; frame rules refuse `owner_ipc_protocol`: a hello of another version (the owner sends its hello, then closes; the client's connect rejects `version_mismatch`, which the election does not retry), a call before the hello (`hello_missing`), an id still open on the connection (`duplicate_id`, the first call keeps its cancel and close abort), arguments that are not a list (`args_not_array`), and an answer the client cannot decode (`result_undecodable`, that call only); the socket directory and lock path resolve symlinks, so every alias of one brain names one owner). When the owner exits, a call in flight ends `owner_unavailable` with reason `outcome_unknown`; a RustEngine client (`installOwnerClient`'s `rejoin`, `RustEngine._takeOver`, which shares the open's single flight) runs the election again on its next call and becomes the owner, opening its store, or a client of the new owner, so the call works with no restart; a whole operation that finds this process became the owner ends `not_sent` and its retry runs in process, while a `put_page` forwarded that way runs its handler in process at once. A client installed without `rejoin` answers later calls `not_sent`. Pinned by `test/rust-owner-ipc.test.ts` (child processes from `test/helpers/owner-ipc-child.ts`), `test/rust-owner-failover.test.ts` (an owner killed mid-write, real addon), `test/rust-owner-ipc-frames.test.ts`, `test/rust-owner-ipc-limits.test.ts`, `test/rust-owner-ipc-peer.test.ts` and `test/rust-owner-ipc-remote.test.ts`. `whole-op.ts` also carries two whole operations that are not imports: `snapshotStore` and `snapshotStatus`, run by `src/core/rust-engine/store-snapshot.ts` on the owner. A snapshot copies the brain at one committed generation (`rust/docs/store/snapshot.md`), one at a time per store; a caller that is gone, a free-space reserve of max(2 GiB, 5 %) and a 2-hour age budget each cancel it, and the call settles, and `snapshotStatus(opId)` says terminal, only after the Rust call has returned with the read released and the target removed. Only the trusted local caller may run it, in process as well as before it is forwarded (a remote caller is refused `engine_capability_missing`). A copy that does not run or finish ends `rust_snapshot_stopped` with reason `busy`, `cancelled`, `disk_pressure` or `age_budget`; a copy asked for while the engine is disconnecting (`RustEngine.disconnecting`) is refused with reason `cancelled` before the Rust call starts, and a disconnect stops a copy already running and waits for the Rust call before the store closes. No entry point calls the two operations yet. `verifyStoreDir` in `store.ts` checks a copy without opening it. Pinned by `test/rust-engine-store-snapshot.test.ts`.

The module also holds the SQL fragments of those holds (`overdueDeferredFrom`, `overdueImportFrom`, `holdOverdue`), which the owner's idle probe (`overdueDeferredWorkSql`) reuses, so the text is the one the callers always sent, byte for byte. Both engines send it unscoped through their own executor; the hold check runs on the transaction's connection. The Rust engine serves them in `src/core/rust-engine/store-members/owner-scans.ts` from `gbrain-store` `persistence/owner_scans.rs`: a Rust brain has no persistence worktrees, so every list is empty and no hold stands, and an id that is not a uuid gets the Postgres uuid refusal. Pinned by `test/engine-parity/owner-recovery-scans.test.ts`.

- `rust/crates/gbrain-store/src/keyword/cjk.rs` — the Rust store's CJK keyword path, the port of `src/core/search/cjk-keyword-sql.ts` as PGLite runs it (`engine-sql/cjk-search.ts`): `Store::search_keyword_cjk` (napi `searchKeywordCjk`) answers `searchKeyword` (best chunk per page) and `searchKeywordChunks`, and `CjkMatcher` serves the paging members' `cjkTerms`. The TS member sends the match terms with their operator (LIKE, or ILIKE for a term with case, folded by `pg_lower`); the store scans the text chunks and reads no vector or posting. Strict AND, the `orFallback` any-term ranking (`1000 × matched + score`) and the bounded `cjkKeyword` arm (an exact chunk count against `pgliteCappedChunks`, candidates by ascending chunk id). Pinned by its unit tests and `test/engine-parity/search-cjk.test.ts`; rust/docs/store/keyword.md ("CJK queries") lists the differences.
- `src/core/engine-sql/trust-fence.ts` + `rust/crates/gbrain-store/src/trust/held_rows.rs` — the fence guard's read of a page's facts-fence rows with their trust tier (`listFenceFactRows`, from `trust/fence-guard.ts` `guardFenceRows`): live numbered rows the incoming fence does not state as they are, that are more trusted than the writer (`gbrain.write_trust_tier`, read by the statement; `WRITER_RANK_SQL`), held narrower by a widening proposal, or opened by `gbrain repair open-facts`, locked `FOR UPDATE OF f`. The incoming fence is bound once as `v.incoming`. `src/core/engine-sql/facts.ts` `listFenceRetireCandidates` is the fence projection's read of the rows it would retire (before-state as `FACT_PREIMAGE_SQL`, from `generations/adapters/fact-rows.ts`). The Rust store answers both from its write trust context, with each row's stored tier and the held arm read from its `trust_proposals` family (rust/docs/store/facts.md). `listHeldRows` is the held-row read of `trust/fence-guard.ts` `heldRows` as a typed member on every engine: of one page's `takes` or `timeline_entries` rows among the given ids, those more trusted than the writer (a named tier, else `gbrain.write_trust_tier`) or, with `pending`, under a pending `forget` contest (`OPEN_CONTEST_SQL`), `{ id, trust_tier }` by id, locked `FOR UPDATE OF x`. Callers pass the page id: projection (`canonical-projections.ts`), the timeline orphan prune (`timeline-extract.ts`) and takes reconcile (`cycle/extract-takes.ts`). The Rust store serves `timeline_entries` from its stamped tier and the `trust_proposals` family; its TypeScript member and the store refuse `takes`, whose records keep no tier. Test: `test/engine-parity/trust-fence.test.ts`.
