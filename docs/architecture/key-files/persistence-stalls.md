# Managed sync and persistence stalls (key files cluster)

[Subsystem index](../KEY_FILES.md).

Bounded write preparation (#6278: budgets, attempt counter, hard ceiling,
`preparation_stalled`), claim phases and steps and the `persistence_write_stall`
doctor check (#6176), releasing a source claimed before activation (#6122), the
`concurrent_write` sync hold (#6194), Git-scoped writer manifests (#6099) and the
shared Git checkout classifier (#6210). The modules they extend (sync-run,
sync-holds, sync-drain, deactivation, source lifecycle) are in
[core persistence](core-persistence.md) and
[Google and loops (continued)](google-and-loops-continued.md).

The managed sync reliability contract (#6317) adds one consumer per host as a
preference (`persistence_consumers` heartbeat rows, waiter-only consumers,
`persistence.single_consumer`), a `drain_stalled` that can fire on a same-host
owner (`owner_wedged_here`), movement surfaces (`data_moving`, doctor
`managed_sync_not_moving`, `gbrain sources writer movement`) and the
`host_identity_mismatch` check; its codes are registered in
`src/core/error-registry.ts` and pinned by `test/error-registry-6317-contract.test.ts`.

- `src/core/persistence/claim-phase.ts` — #6176 / #6278: the phase and step a running write request's claim is in. `startClaimPhase`/`enterClaimPhase` keep a per-claim `ClaimPhaseClock` in the consumer (single and group routes); `claimPhaseStamp` is the jsonb every claim, renewal and group dispatch mark stores in `persistence_requests.claim_phase` (v217): phase `preparing` or `publishing`, claim start, phase start, the execution token (so a later claim never reads an earlier claim's ages), the `step` in flight and when it began, `waiting_on` and the owning process (`owner`: command kind, pid, gbrain build). Preparers (`sync-prepare.ts`, `page-prepare.ts`, `prepared-maintenance.ts`, link resolution) call `enterClaimStep(clock, step, signal, waitingOn)` at each await boundary: it names the step, records what the await waits on (`git`, `fs`, `db`, `pool`, else `unknown`: set only where observed, never inferred) and throws the preparation's abort reason, so cancellation reaches a preparer between its queries even though the queries take no signal. `claimStateOf` reads a row back from the current claim's stamp only: `preparing`/`publishing` (live renewal), `publication_transaction` (claim lapsed while running, read on the database clock: a transaction holds the row or the owner is gone; requeued once it ends), `file_publication` (`publication_started`; recovery finishes it) or `unrecorded`, with claim and phase ages, `step`, `step_age_ms`, `waiting_on`, `owner`, `resumes_on_its_own` and a `why`. `claimStall` is the `preparation_overdue` verdict (`claim.stall`) for a preparing claim past its budget. `EXPIRED_PREPARING_CHARGE_SQL` is the one rule an expired-claim reclaim charges a preparation attempt by: only a claim stamped `preparing` under the token being reclaimed. `persistence.max_claim_ms` (default 600000, 60000 to 86400000; `validateMaxClaimConfigValue` is `config set`'s `invalid_params` refusal) is doctor `persistence_write_stall`'s threshold. Consumer and group-publish store the stamp on every renewal, so writer status (`diagnostics.ts`, `claim` on each running blocker, never the token) and doctor name a stuck phase and step without an extra statement per write. Tests: `test/persistence-write-stall.test.ts`, `test/persistence-claim-step.test.ts`.
- `src/core/persistence/preparation-budget.ts` + `src/commands/config/preparation-keys.ts` — #6278: how long a claimed write may prepare and how many cut-offs it survives. `preparationKind` sorts a request into `foreground` (`remember`, and `put_page`/`edit_page` with no internal intent: the consumer's 30 s `preparationMs`), `sync` (`managed_sync_*` kinds: `persistence.sync_preparation_ms`, default 120000) or `maintenance` (every other kind: `persistence.maintenance_preparation_ms`, default 120000). `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms` (default 600000, 60000 to 3600000, floored at the larger budget plus 30 s) is the hard ceiling for a preparation that ignores cancellation and `persistence.max_preparation_attempts` (default 2, 1 to 10) the cut-offs before a request finishes `preparation_stalled`; a missing or out-of-range value keeps its default (`resolvePreparationPolicy`). The four keys ride the write-switch snapshot (`switches.ts`, one statement, 5 s TTL) together with the `preparation_deadlines` kill switch, so a claim never reads config on its own. `startPreparation` runs a preparer under its budget and the budget wins the race even when the preparer ignores its signal (the signal aborts with `{ code: 'preparation_deadline' }`; a result that lands after the budget on the clock still reads as a deadline; the late result is the caller's to drop); `racePreparation` is its outcome-only form for the sync's out-of-consumer preparations (`sync-waivers.ts`). `validatePreparationConfigValue` is `config set`'s structured refusal (each key's range, and the ceiling rule across the sibling keys; usage error exit 2, nothing written), wired in by `refuseInvalidPreparationValue`. `persistence.max_claim_ms` keeps its doctor meaning. Tests: `test/persistence-preparation-budget.test.ts`.
- `src/core/persistence/bounded-reads.ts` — #6278 (plan item 1.4): a preparation read that can wait on a relation lock ends at the budget on the server. Phase 0 (`docs/eval/managed-sync-stall-repro.md`) showed that through a transaction-mode pooler gbrain's `statement_timeout` startup parameter is dropped, so a preparation read outside the publication transaction had no server-side bound; an `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` lock on `pages` held elsewhere pinned the member's origin check and its connection until the lock lifted, and with the budget alone the released member's statement kept running as a zombie that blocked its root until the ceiling. `boundedReads(engine, clock)` is the engine view every preparer reads through (`prepareManagedSyncMutation` and its helpers, `preparePageMutation`, `prepareFactFenceAdoption`, the sync's out-of-consumer preparations): each unmemoized raw statement runs with `executeRaw`'s `timeoutMs` set to the clock's remaining budget (`ClaimPhaseClock.deadlineAt`, set by `startClaimPhase(now, signal, budgetMs)`) and the clock's signal, which ends only the wait for a free connection. On Postgres `timeoutMs` runs the statement on a reserved connection as `BEGIN; SET LOCAL statement_timeout; <statement>; COMMIT` in one pipelined round trip, so the bound holds through the pooler and `SET LOCAL` ends with it; inside a transaction and on PGLite the option is ignored. A statement the server ends (57014, or a 55P03 raised by a shorter session `lock_timeout`) surfaces as the member's own `preparation_deadline` (`PreparationDeadlineError`, or the signal's reason once it aborted), which `preparationAbortReason` classifies as the deadline: the consumer and the group route handle it as the deadline even inside the budget (charged release, `preparation_stalled` at the limit, the group cut like the timer), never as a `storage_error` receipt or an uncharged release. The memo rule holds: `preparationReads` answers a bounded read once with the member's bound and signal dropped. Engine helpers that bypass `executeRaw` (`readPageSnapshot`, the import pipeline) stay under the consumer's race and the ceiling. Tests: `test/persistence-bounded-reads.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-preparation-lock-wait-postgres.test.ts` (a real lock held in another session), the server-ended cases in `test/persistence-consumer-scheduling.test.ts` and `test/e2e/persistence-group-deadline-postgres.test.ts`. GBRA-75 wave 9: `withBoundedReadSession` gives a waiver screen (`unchangedSyncImport`) one reserved ordinary-pool connection (a long-hold permit; none left, or PGLite, runs the screen as before) on which its bounded reads share one transaction: `BEGIN; SET LOCAL statement_timeout` once, `COMMIT` when the screen settles. The bound stays per statement on the server: it is set again in the read's pipeline once the bound in force differs from the read's own by more than `BOUNDED_SESSION_SLACK_MS` (25 ms). A waiver run (`waiveRun` in `sync-run.ts`) opens one session for all its screens, and a screen inside it joins it. Session reads are named prepared statements (`ReservedConnection.executeRaw` `prepare`, ANDed with the connection option as `runUnsafe` does). A failed read rolls back and the reads it aborted (25P02) run again in a new transaction; a read after the screen settled takes the per-read path. Test: `test/persistence-bounded-read-session.test.ts` (real lock wait and no remaining statement on Postgres).

Used by `maintenancePreflight`, `loadFenceSource` and `managedRepairRoot`; the registry's `owner_unavailable.reasons` and the `FENCE_REASONS` owner entries carry the same vocabulary. - `src/core/persistence/repair-busy.ts` — #6278: the busy set a repair must not write while a managed sync runs. `loadRepairBusySet(engine, sourceId, incarnation)` returns the paths and slugs named by queued, running and recovering requests and terminal rows with an open recovery (both rename endpoints included) plus, for every unfinished `managed-sync` cursor of the incarnation, the entries of its frozen `managed-sync-manifest` from the cursor's index on (a path with a `sync-hold` record is left out: the sync retries a held file only when its bytes change); it never throws and fails closed (`unknown` set, so `repairBusy` answers true for everything) when a query fails or a cursor's manifest is missing. `repairBusyMessage` is the hold text, `repairBusyError` the `sync_in_progress` refusal (`reason` `candidate_in_flight` or `busy_set_unreadable`) `submitManagedFileRepair` raises at admission. - `src/core/persistence/consumer.ts` — resident canonical-write consumer with retained wake-ups, root-scoped retry backoff and locked-row-skipping expired-claim sweeps. A full tick fans out to every worker; when idle, each tick instead runs one work probe (a single statement mirroring the claim, recovery, expired-claim, effect, projection and topology predicates, excluding rows those workers would skip, including topology recoveries that failed to finish within the last 30 s) and the poll interval doubles from 250 ms to a 5 s cap (`idleMaxMs`), resetting on work. `wake()` runs a full tick immediately; `waitForWrite` calls it after a same-process admission commits, so cross-process writes wait at most 5 s plus one probe and no LISTEN is used. #5984: `awaitWrite` (`waitForWrite` is its row-only form) receives a request this consumer publishes through the `onSettled` handoff with no request read, and skips polls while `holds(id)` says the consumer has it claimed; otherwise it polls the narrow `WRITE_PROGRESS_SQL` (first poll after min(200 ms, half the wait), then 50 ms backing off to 250 ms) and reads the full row once when terminal. Its own cancelled reads are not failures; a database read failure ends the wait as a classified `read_failed` (auth/permission failures at once), and a pending head with an intervention `blocked_reason` ends as `blocked`. A probe failure is reported once and the next tick runs the full fan-out. On Postgres pools of three or more with no connection checked out, idle probes share one reserved connection from the ordinary pool, never the direct/session route (acquired and queried under the phase deadline; a timed-out lane returns to the pool once its query settles) so the rest of the pool drains through the 20 s `idle_timeout` (postgres.js reuses idle connections first-in-first-out); root refresh and receipt compaction run every 5 minutes while idle. Root refresh, recovery scan, sweep and capacity-marking SQL use cooperative five-second cancellation; failure never admits work under stale confinement. Cold direct initialization and queued transactions remain observed and awaited through shutdown. Recognized cancellation owned by the consumer's stop reason is not reported as a storage failure; deadline-first cancellation and unrelated storage errors remain visible. The rate-limited stderr line names the phase, the SQLSTATE or write-error code, a redacted one-line message of at most 200 characters, connection-wait evidence (`first_conn_ms`, or `checkout=not_observed conn_wait_ms`, attributed to the phase through AsyncLocalStorage over the engine's `onCheckout` seam) plus `loop_lag_ms`, a fix command and its docs anchor (`docs/ENGINES.md#persistence-consumer-log`); the trusted status `phase` carries the same fields. Every claimed write has a preparation budget (`preparation-budget.ts`): `execute` races the preparation against it (`startPreparation`), so the claim is released with `blocked_reason` `preparation_deadline` and one counted attempt at the budget even when the preparer ignores its signal, and a late result never publishes (the execution-token fence). A request already at `persistence.max_preparation_attempts` is finished `failed` with `preparation_stalled` at claim without preparing again, and the release that reaches the limit finishes it there (`coordinator.ts` `finishPreparationStalled`; a waiting `awaitWrite` caller gets the terminal receipt, foreground writes included); a preparer's rejection on the consumer's own abort (its reason, an `AbortError` or a cancelled statement, `preparationAbortReason`) is a release, never a receipt. The expired-claim sweep (Postgres, and PGLite's `releaseAbandonedClaims`) charges one attempt for a claim stamped `preparing` (a process killed mid-preparation); `claim_lost`, `consumer_stopping` and `group_member_waiting` never charge, and a commit resets the counter. The #5373 root barrier for an abandoned preparation ends at `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms` measured from the claim: the root is freed, the counter is pinned at the limit (an overrun proves cancellation is ignored, so the next claim fails the request `preparation_stalled`) and the zombie stays in `status().outlived_ceiling`; at the per-process cap (`zombieCap`, default 1) the consumer claims nothing more and `status().restart_required` is true. `stop()` gives abandoned preparations `ABANDONED_STOP_GRACE_MS` (5 s) after aborting them instead of waiting forever. The fault point `consumer:preparing` sits inside the budget, `status()` reports `preparation_policy` and `abandoned_preparations`, and the stderr line carries `phase=preparation reason=deadline_exceeded | preparation_stalled | ceiling_exceeded`. With the `preparation_deadlines` switch off only `remember` and intent-less `put_page`/`edit_page` keep the 30 s budget, the old unbounded wait applies and no attempt is counted. Publication and shutdown retain the token/native-lock/recovery barriers. Trusted status exposes sampled process-local phases and attempts, not durable progress. A tick woken by a completed publication or a local admission claims the next write at once and runs root refresh plus recovery, expiry and topology scans at most once per poll interval; explicit, timer and idle-probe ticks always scan. Effects drain in batches of 20 without waiting for the next tick while full batches keep arriving; within a batch, git effects wait for their asynchronous durability probe (one per worktree root) after the other effects, and a single-file git effect in a repository without the durability hook acquires the worktree lock only to prove its root and records its outcome after releasing it. Each projection-worker invocation (`runResidentProjectionInvocation`, #5401) rebuilds up to 100 pages and starts no new page once 250 ms have passed since it began; when `hasClaimableWrite` (the `claimableWriteSql` predicate `claimNextWrite` claims with, in `journal.ts`) finds a claimable write at its start it takes 2 pages, and its selector skips failed rows for 30 s through the same `PROJECTION_RETRY_READY_SQL` fragment as the idle probe (measured PGLite rebuild cost on a 1,000-page fixture: mean ~9.5 ms, p99 ~18 ms per page). On PGLite the first full tick releases claims last written before this process started (`releaseAbandonedClaims` in `effect-journal.ts`: running effects and unpublished requests without a recovery record), since the datastore admits one process and those owners have exited; Postgres keeps lease expiry (requests 30 s, effects 2 minutes). On PGLite, which has no autovacuum, the maintenance worker also runs `vacuumPersistenceQueues` (VACUUM ANALYZE of the queue tables) every minute or after 50 + 20% of the request table's rows in publications. Tests: `persistence-consumer-scheduling.test.ts`, `persistence-consumer-idle.test.ts`, `persistence-consumer-log.test.ts`, `projection-drain.test.ts` (injected clock and per-page cost), `persistence-root-refresh.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-phase-liveness.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-idle-pool.test.ts`. With `requestsOnly` (engine graduation's drain) the effect, projection, topology and maintenance workers never start; claim, recovery, expired-claim and publication run as usual and `stop()` remains the barrier. Both the single route and the grouped/lane route (`executeClaimedGroup` in `group-publish.ts`) prepare under a claim lease (`claim-lease.ts`: a renewal every `renewalIntervalMs`, default 10 s, each bounded by `phaseMs`); the claim is lost the first time a renewal says it is gone, throws or overruns, and `whileHeld` stops waiting on the preparation at that moment (the single route also aborts it). The claims still ours are released unpublished with `claim_lost` (token-fenced, so a consumer that took one over keeps it); the abandoned preparation is never published and keeps its root or lane slot until it settles or the ceiling passes, while the consumer slot frees at once. Renewals are never awaited on the way out: `stop()` drains in-flight renewals and gives abandoned preparations their grace before engine close. Tests: `test/persistence-claim-lease.test.ts`, `test/persistence-consumer-scheduling.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-consumer-claim-loss.test.ts`, `test/persistence-preparation-kill-accounting.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-preparation-deadline.e2e.test.ts`. #6355: `onLane` lends its warm lane's transaction through a wrapper that notes a connection loss (`isConnectionLoss`); in `finally` a lost lane is released and `idleLaneRetryAt` set a minute out, whatever `run` returned or threw (admission turns the loss into `write_outcome_unknown` or succeeds on the pool, so the catch alone never saw it), because the dead reservation answers every later statement with "Connection is no longer owned" (`test/persistence-consumer-lane-loss.test.ts`).
- **`src/core/persistence/sync-group.ts`** + **`group-publish.ts`** — #5984 bulk publication for draining managed Postgres syncs. `sync-group.ts` resolves bulk settings (`--no-bulk` > `GBRAIN_SYNC_BULK` > `sync.bulk` > on; `sync.bulk_size` 16, `sync.bulk_max_txn_ms` 15 s; PGLite off), sizes the next group from the last group's admission-to-commit time per member, freezes followers (four at a time, stopping at a rename, company plan, overtaken entry or one the waiver would take) and admits a group in one transaction (`admitWriteGroupInTransaction`, which keeps one request row, ID and digest per page and replays priors). `sync-run.ts` keeps the head in `pending` and all members in the cursor's `group` (members carry `intent.group` = the head's request ID), waits for the last member and advances over the committed prefix; a terminal failure becomes the single pending entry, a still-pending member returns `writer_pending`. Any caller finishes an existing group; only a drain forms one. #6075: when another owner loop already admitted the group's head on the single path with the head's pre-group intent (the intent minus the `group`/`lane` keys `formGroup` adds, `groupedIntent`/`ungroupedIntent`), `groupStep` drops the group and the single path takes that request, and the never-admitted followers are frozen again; a different intent under the head's request ID still refuses `idempotency_conflict`. The single path's ENG-A7 admission guard compares the stored pending intent as well as its request ID, so a pass that read the head before a drain grouped it re-reads the cursor instead of admitting the stale intent, and an `idempotency_conflict` against a head the group admitted first re-reads the cursor too (`test/managed-sync-stale-group-head.test.ts`). The consumer claims a head's directly following queued members (`claimGroupFollowers`, never past an unfinished non-member) and `executeClaimedGroup` prepares four at a time, then `publishGroup` publishes them in one transaction: one worktree lock, ownership guard and coordinated write (each member set as the attributed actor with `setMemberAttribution`), per-member authorization, preimage read, sync validation (its source-wide part shared per transaction), apply (which reads the postimage the receipt and effects use), effects and receipt; `completeGroup` then reads every member's effect bytes, and pipelines the counter lock with one `UPDATE ... FROM unnest(...) RETURNING` that checks each member's claim and terminal reservation and releases the counters (a short `RETURNING` throws, so the single path reports each member's own code). Any failure rolls the group back; members then publish singly, later members are cancelled after a terminal failure or released after a requeue. Sync validation accepts a group member through the cursor's `group`. #6007: the pages of one `put_pages` batch (`intent.page_batch.id`; the group key is `journal.ts` `publicationGroupKey`) and the files of one managed import batch (`intent.import_batch`, key `import:`, grouped on PGLite too; `independentGroup` names both) also publish as groups of at most `PAGE_BATCH_GROUP_MAX` (8) pages, prepared all at once, files included: one transaction records every member's recovery record (`prepareRecoveries`) before any file is touched, the publication transaction marks the members `publication_started`, publishes each member's file before its apply and re-arms the batch's mention links once after every member completes (`reconcileFinishedBatch`), and one transaction clears the records after commit (`clearResolvedRecoveries`). A single foreground page write publishes here too, as a group of one (core-persistence-continued.md, `switches.ts` entry). Within that transaction the source's local path, the owner's host binding and config values are read once per group (source membership and the writer registration are already memoized per transaction by `transactionMemo`). On failure every file the group published is restored by `recoverPublication`, which requeues those claims; the fallback claims them back in order (`reclaimReleasedWrite`) and publishes the members singly, and a batch member's failure never cancels its siblings. Each member has its own preparation budget (#6278): `executeClaimedGroup` marks a wave durably (`markDispatched`) and starts each member's clock when the wave dispatches it, so a follower waiting behind four others is never charged; the lease renews the mutable set of members still held (`renewGroupClaims` stamps each member's own phase), so releasing one member never reads as a lost group lease. When member k's budget passes, nothing later is dispatched, the in-flight suffix is aborted, k is released `preparation_deadline` (charged; finished `preparation_stalled` when that reaches the limit) and the later members `group_member_waiting` (not charged), and the contiguous prepared prefix 0..k-1 publishes (singly when shorter than two; under lanes in the original group's key order). An independent `put_pages` batch group releases only k, `laneFallback` keeps the expired member's reason, and an abandoned member's preparation goes to the consumer with its clock, so the root waits for it until it settles or the ceiling. A member's signal reaches its preparer through its clock (`enterClaimStep`), never as a blanket statement signal: `preparationReads` and `groupReads` answer one read for every member and skip their memo for a signalled statement, so a member that must give up on a shared read races its own signal against the shared promise (pinned by `test/persistence-preparation-memo-rule.test.ts`). A commit resets `preparation_attempts` (`completeGroup`). Tests: `test/managed-sync-bulk.test.ts`, `test/e2e/put-pages-group-postgres.test.ts`, `test/e2e/persistence-group-deadline-postgres.test.ts`.

- `src/core/persistence/consumer-heartbeat.ts` — #6317 (B1a, B3): the probe and heartbeat behind one full consumer per host. `consumerIdentity()` is this process's `{pid, nonce, pid_ns}` (a random per-process nonce and the pid-namespace inode from `/proc/self/ns/pid` where readable), so a reused pid after a container restart is a new row; every `owner_pid === process.pid` check compares the nonce too. `startConsumerHeartbeat(engine, row, opts)` renews this process's `persistence_consumers` row every 10 s on its own single-flight timer through `executeRawDirect` (independent of claim renewal: an idle `serve` holds no claim), carrying kind (`claimOwner().kind`), mode (`probing|full|waiter_only|promoted`), `restart_required`, the age of the oldest abandoned root barrier, the pool gauge `{checked_out, max, waiters}`, `host_json_path`, `persistence_home`, `minted_under` and the build; each renewal deletes rows lapsed over 60 s in the same CTE, and a failed write is logged once and never stops the consumer. `probeLiveFullConsumer(engine, hostId, self)` is the database-only probe (never a socket: the persistence IPC socket exists only on PGLite): a row of a resident kind (`serve`, `sync`, `jobs`, `autopilot`, `mcp`), mode `full`, not this process, renewed within 30 s, not `restart_required` and not wedged (no root barrier older than `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms`), on an `active` binding. `listHostConsumers(engine, hostId)` feeds `writer status --json` `host.consumers` and doctor.
