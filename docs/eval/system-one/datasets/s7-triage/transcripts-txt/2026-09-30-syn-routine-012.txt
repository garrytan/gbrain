What should I look for on the charger or packaging to identify compatibility?

[assistant]
Look for protocol and certification markings near the output specifications. Common wording includes:

- USB Power Delivery or “USB-PD”
- Programmable Power Supply or “PPS”
- Quick Charge or another device-specific charging standard
- A profile table showing fixed voltages and currents
- A USB-C logo or a stated USB-C power rating
- Safety and certification marks, plus a model number

Also check the cable. A USB-C charger may need a suitable USB-C-to-USB-C cable for USB-PD operation; a lower-rated or different cable can limit the available current or prevent a particular mode. The phone’s manual or specifications should list the charging standard and maximum supported input.

If neither package identifies a protocol, the printed voltage/current table still supports the basic comparison, but it does not establish that the phone will request the highest profile.

It can be useful for a simple comparison, but it is not necessary if the packaging and phone already provide enough information. A basic USB-C power meter can display negotiated voltage, current, and sometimes power. For example, it might show:

or:

9.08 V × 2.10 A = 19.1 W

For a meaningful test, use the same cable, the same phone battery range, and similar temperature conditions. Test each charger separately, with the phone screen off if practical, and record the reading after a minute or two rather than immediately after plugging in.

Check that the meter supports USB-C Power Delivery and the voltage range you intend to measure. Some inexpensive meters are intended only for basic USB-A measurements or can interfere with negotiation.
