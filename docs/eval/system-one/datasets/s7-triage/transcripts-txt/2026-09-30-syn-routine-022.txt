[user]
The label says:

“Water when top 1–2 inches of soil are dry. Keep evenly moist, but never soggy. Reduce watering in winter. Bright, indirect light. Do not allow plant to stand in water.”

It’s in a 6-inch plastic nursery pot with one drainage hole. It’s on a shelf about 4 feet from an east-facing window. The room is usually 68–72°F. I don’t know the plant species.

[assistant]
That is enough for a cautious starting schedule, although the soil check matters more than the calendar. For a 6-inch pot, try this:

- Check the soil every 4 days at first.
- Water only when the top 1–2 inches feel dry.
- When watering, pour slowly until a small amount drains from the hole.
- Empty the saucer or outer container after about 10 minutes.
- If the top is still damp at the check, wait 2 days and check again.
- In winter, begin checking every 6–7 days instead, since the plant may use water more slowly.

The east-facing location may provide moderate bright light, but distance, curtains, and window exposure can change that. Avoid using a fixed amount such as one cup until you see how quickly the soil dries.

[user]
I’d prefer something I can put on my calendar. Could I start with watering every Sunday?

[assistant]
You can use Sunday as a check-in day, but I would not automatically water every Sunday. A weekly reminder to test the soil fits the label better than a weekly watering rule.

For the first few weeks, use this calendar entry:

“Sunday: Check top 1–2 inches of soil. Water slowly until a little drains if dry; otherwise check again Wednesday.”

If the soil is still damp every Sunday and Wednesday, change the reminder to once weekly or every 8–10 days. If it is dry before Sunday, add a Wednesday check. The calendar provides the prompt, while the soil determines whether water is needed.

[user]
Can you make that more concrete? I tend to forget what “a little drains” means, and I don’t want to overwater it.

[assistant]
Use this simple procedure:

1. Insert a finger about 2 inches into the soil near the pot’s edge.
2. If the soil feels cool and distinctly damp, do not water.
3. If it feels mostly dry and loose, water.
4. Pour about 250 milliliters slowly over 30–60 seconds.
5. Wait 2 minutes. If no water comes through the drainage hole, add another 100 milliliters.
6. Stop once a few drops or a narrow trickle appear below the pot.
7. Let the pot drain for 10 minutes, then empty collected water.

The 250-milliliter amount is only a starting point for this pot size. If the soil is very dry and water runs straight down the sides, pause for 5 minutes and apply another small pour rather than adding a large amount all at once.

[user]
The pot sits inside a decorative container, so I can’t easily see the drainage hole. How should I handle that?

[assistant]
Take the nursery pot out of the decorative container before watering. Place it in a sink, bathtub, shower, or shallow tray. Water it there using the slow-pour procedure, and leave it out for about 10 minutes after drainage stops.

Before returning it, tilt the nursery pot slightly and confirm that no water is pooled underneath. If the decorative container has no drainage hole, never leave the nursery pot standing in collected water. A thin layer of gravel in the outer container does not replace drainage, so removing the nursery pot is the safer routine.

I checked today, and the top 2 inches are dry, but the soil underneath feels slightly damp. The leaves look normal. Should I water now or wait?

[assistant]
Wait for now. The label says to water when the top 1–2 inches are dry, but “keep evenly moist” and “never soggy” make the transition area important. Since the lower soil is still slightly damp, check again in 2 days.

At the next check, test in two spots:

- Near the edge, about 2 inches deep.
- Halfway between the edge and the stem, about 2 inches deep.

If both spots feel mostly dry, water in the sink. If either spot feels distinctly damp, wait another 1–2 days. Do not use leaf appearance alone to decide, since normal-looking leaves can occur across several soil-moisture levels.

What if I accidentally pour too much and water collects in the decorative container?

[assistant]
Remove the nursery pot from the decorative container immediately and let it drain in a sink or tray. Empty the collected water from the decorative container, then leave the nursery pot out for 10–15 minutes.

If the potting mix is thoroughly saturated but the plant is upright and the leaves look normal, do not add anything else. Resume checking the soil after several days, but wait to water until the top 2 inches are dry and the deeper soil is no longer distinctly wet.

For future watering, use a measuring cup, keep the nursery pot outside the decorative container, and pour in two small stages. This makes it easier to stop when drainage begins.

One more question: should I mist the leaves between waterings?

[assistant]
Misting is not needed for the schedule described on the label. It does not substitute for watering the soil, and frequent misting can leave nearby surfaces wet without reliably changing the root-zone moisture.

Keep the leaves dry unless you are wiping off dust. If you wipe them, use a clean, lightly damp cloth and avoid rubbing hard. Continue with the soil checks, slow watering, drainage, and emptying the decorative container.
