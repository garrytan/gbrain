Yes, mathematically 2 1/4 × 250 mL equals 562.5 mL. However, there are two separate issues:

1. Recipe cups may not mean the same physical volume. A US customary cup is about 236.6 mL, while a metric cup is often 250 mL. The original recipe’s “cup” convention is not stated.
2. Flour is affected by how it is filled. Scooping directly from the bag can compact it, while spooning it into the cup and leveling it is lighter and more repeatable.

If the original recipe was written with US cups, 2 1/4 US cups corresponds to about 532 mL when expressed as physical volume. If you use a 250 mL cup and want to reproduce that physical volume, measure about 2 cups plus 1 tablespoon and 1 teaspoon, using the same level-fill method each time. If the original recipe was written for metric cups, use 2 1/4 of your 250 mL cups.

Without a scale, I’d use the recipe’s intended cup system if known. If not, use a level-fill method and avoid packing the flour. The gram estimate of about 270 g is generally the more useful target, but volume alone cannot guarantee that amount.

Can you convert the original 375°F oven temperature to Celsius, and tell me whether I should change the baking time for 18 muffins?

[assistant]
375°F converts to approximately 190.6°C, usually rounded to 190°C or 191°C. Set a conventional oven to 190°C. If your oven uses fan/convection heat, a common equivalent is about 170°C fan, though ovens vary.

Because the individual muffins are the same size, scaling from 12 to 18 does not automatically require a longer bake. The main change is the number of muffins and possibly the arrangement of the pan. Bake at the same temperature and begin checking at 18 minutes. They may need roughly 18–24 minutes if the pan is more crowded or the oven recovers slowly. They are done when the tops spring back lightly and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs.

If you bake two trays at once, rotate and swap their positions around the halfway point if your oven has uneven heating. Avoid opening the door repeatedly; check once near the lower end of the time range.

[assistant]
No ingredient adjustment is needed, but handle the waiting batter gently:

- Fill the first 12 muffin wells, leaving the remaining batter covered at room temperature while the first batch bakes.
- Do not refrigerate it unless the room is unusually warm; a cold batter can bake differently and may have less rise.
- If the batter contains baking powder and baking soda, the second batch may rise slightly less after sitting, especially if the batter is mixed a lot or left for a long time.
- Keep the blueberries distributed evenly. If they tend to sink, fold them in just before portioning, but avoid stirring the batter after the first batch goes into the oven.
- Set aside enough batter for six muffins before baking, or portion by using the same scoop count for each muffin.

If the batter will wait longer than about 30–40 minutes, you can refrigerate it covered, then portion it without vigorously stirring. Add a minute or two to the second batch if needed, checking by appearance and with a toothpick.
