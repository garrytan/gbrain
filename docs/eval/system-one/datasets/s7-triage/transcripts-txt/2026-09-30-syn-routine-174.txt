[assistant]
No. A higher wattage rating by itself does not make Charger B safer, and it does not automatically make it riskier.

With standard USB-C Power Delivery, the phone and charger negotiate a voltage and current profile. The charger advertises what it can provide, and the phone requests a suitable level. A phone that normally charges around 18W should generally request only a supported profile, assuming the charger and cable are compliant and operating normally.

For your two labels, both chargers list 5V at 3A, and both list a 9V option. Charger A’s 9V option is about 20W; Charger B’s is 27W. If the phone requests roughly 18W, the charger’s extra capacity is normally unused.

Safety depends more on factors such as:
- Whether the charger is genuine and undamaged
- Whether its safety certifications are credible
- Whether the cable is suitable and undamaged
- Whether the connector fits securely
- Whether the charger becomes unusually hot, buzzes, smells odd, or cuts out

The “Quick Charge” wording also does not by itself prove compatibility or quality. If you want a conservative choice while checking the phone’s specifications, Charger A is sufficient for an approximately 18W phone, while B is not inherently unsafe solely because it is rated higher.

[assistant]
PDO means “Power Data Object.” In a USB Power Delivery charger’s capability list, each PDO describes a fixed voltage and the maximum current available at that voltage.

Your chargers list these fixed PDOs:

Charger A:
- 5V at up to 3A: 15W
- 9V at up to 2.22A: about 20W
- 12V at up to 1.5A: 18W

Charger B:
- 5V at up to 3A: 15W
- 9V at up to 3A: 27W
- 12V at up to 2.5A: 30W

PPS means “Programmable Power Supply.” Instead of selecting only one fixed voltage such as 5V or 9V, a compatible phone can request an adjustable voltage within the listed range, in small steps, while the charger regulates it. B’s PPS range is 3.3–11V up to 3A.

The manual you quoted says the phone supports USB Power Delivery, but it does not say that it supports PPS. Unless the phone’s specifications separately mention PPS, you should not count on the PPS line providing an advantage. The phone can still use an ordinary fixed PD profile, likely 9V/2A, with either charger.

Also, B’s PPS range does not change the phone’s stated 18W maximum. It may be useful for another compatible device, but it is not evidence that this phone will charge faster.

I want to test them with the phone and compare actual behavior. What should I record so the comparison is reasonably fair? I can use the same cable and start with the phone at roughly the same battery percentage.

[assistant]
Use the same conditions for both tests. A simple comparison can be done without specialized equipment.

1. Choose the one-meter cable if it appears intact, and use that exact cable for both chargers.
2. Let the phone cool to ordinary room temperature before each test.
3. Close charging-related apps and avoid using the phone during the test.
4. Disable or keep unchanged features such as battery protection, adaptive charging, low-power mode, and optimized charging.
5. Record the starting battery percentage and the approximate room temperature.
6. Connect the first charger and note whether the phone displays “charging,” “fast charging,” or another message.
7. Record the percentage after 10 minutes and again after 30 minutes.
8. Note whether charging pauses, repeatedly reconnects, or produces any warning.
9. Check the charger, cable, and phone connector for unusual heat, without touching anything that seems dangerously hot.
10. Let the phone return to a similar starting percentage and temperature before testing the second charger.

For a fairer result, test each charger twice and compare the average percentage increase over the same interval. Battery percentage readings are rounded, and charging speed normally tapers as the battery fills, so do not treat a single five-minute observation as a precise wattage measurement.

Avoid charging on a bed or under clothing, and do not intentionally cover the phone or charger to see how hot they get.

One last question: if I later use Charger B with a different USB-C device that supports 30W, would its 12V/2.5A line automatically give that device 30W, or would the device still need to request it?

[assistant]
The device would need to request a supported profile; the charger does not automatically force 30W.

In USB Power Delivery, the charger advertises its available PDOs, including B’s 12V/2.5A option. The connected device evaluates those capabilities and sends a request for a profile it supports. If the device supports and requests 12V at up to 2.5A, and the cable and charging conditions are suitable, that profile can provide up to 30W. If it requests 9V/3A, it can use up to 27W instead. It may request less, depending on its battery level, temperature, settings, and internal charging limits.

A device advertised as “30W” might use a different arrangement, such as 15V/2A or a PPS range, rather than 12V/2.5A. Its specifications need to match one of the charger’s profiles. USB-C compatibility of the connector does not guarantee that every advertised charging mode is available.

Also, the device’s own input limit, the cable’s rating, and any protocol requirements matter. A charger having a 30W PDO means it can offer that profile; it does not mean every connected device will use it.
