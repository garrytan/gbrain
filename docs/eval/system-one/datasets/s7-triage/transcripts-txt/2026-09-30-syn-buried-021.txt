A heated towel rail can help, but avoid placing clothing directly on the bars unless the hotel explicitly permits it and the rail is clean and intended for that use. The safest approach is indirect drying:

1. Wash underwear and socks in the sink with a small amount of travel detergent.
2. Rinse until the water runs clear and no soap remains.
3. Press each item between two clean towels. Do not twist hard, since that can stretch elastic.
4. Hang the items on the hotel’s normal towel rail using a few clothespins, an S-hook, or a hanger, so they are suspended with airflow around them rather than lying on the heated surface.
5. Leave space between pieces, keep them away from the thermostat or electrical controls, and do not cover the whole rail with fabric.
6. Keep the bathroom door open if the room is not humid, or move the hanger just outside the bathroom if condensation builds up.
7. Check the items in the morning. If they are not completely dry, do not seal them in your backpack; use the zip bag only as a temporary separate container and finish drying them when possible.

If the rail becomes very hot, smells unusual, or has a warning label, turn it off and use a normal hanger near—not on—the rail instead.
