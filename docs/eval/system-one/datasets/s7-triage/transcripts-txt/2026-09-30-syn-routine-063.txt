Can you also convert the temperature to gas mark, and explain whether I should change the temperature because I’m using a fan oven? The recipe gives 20–25 minutes, but I’m worried the scaled batch will take longer.

[assistant]
375°F is approximately:

- 190°C conventional oven
- Gas Mark 5
- About 170°C fan-assisted

If your recipe’s 375°F setting was intended for a conventional oven and you are using a fan oven, set it to about 170°C fan. Check your oven manual if it uses a different fan conversion.

For 18 muffins, the baking time depends more on the size of each muffin and whether the tins are crowded than on the total amount of batter. If all 18 muffins are baked at once in tins with good air circulation, begin checking around 18–20 minutes at 170°C fan. They are done when the tops spring back lightly and a skewer inserted in the centre comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs.

If you bake in batches, use the same temperature and begin checking at the same point. Avoid leaving mixed batter standing for a long time, particularly with self-raising flour, because some of its raising action begins once the wet and dry ingredients are combined. If your oven holds only one tin comfortably, mixing the full batch and baking two tins consecutively is usually simpler, but fill the second tin just before it goes into the oven if practical.

I have two 12-hole tins, but one is dark nonstick and the other is a light aluminium tin. If I bake all 18 at once, should I arrange them differently or use different timing?

[assistant]
Arrange the tins so air can circulate around each one and neither tin blocks the other. If your oven has two shelves, place one tin on the centre of each shelf, with space above and below. Halfway through baking, swap the tins between shelves and turn each tin front to back if your oven heats unevenly.

The dark nonstick tin usually absorbs more heat, so its muffins may brown and finish slightly sooner than those in the light aluminium tin. Start checking the dark-tin muffins first, perhaps around 18 minutes, and check the light-tin muffins at the same time or a minute or two later. Avoid opening the oven repeatedly before the muffins have set.

At 170°C fan, use the original 20–25 minutes as a guide rather than adding time simply because the batch is larger. If the two tins are crowded or the oven temperature drops noticeably, they may need a few extra minutes. Test individual muffins from both tins, since one tin can be ready before the other. If the tops brown too quickly while the centres are still wet, loosely cover the tins with foil and continue baking.

| Fine salt | 4.5 g (3/4 tsp) | 4.5 g (1 1/2 × 1/2 tsp) |
| Vanilla | 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) | 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) |

Method:

1. Set a fan oven to 170°C, equivalent to about 190°C conventional, 375°F, or Gas Mark 5. Position the two tins with room around them. If using two shelves, place one tin centrally on each shelf.
2. Crack two large eggs into a bowl and beat until uniform. Weigh 75 g of the beaten egg into your mixing bowl. Reserve the rest for another use.
3. Add the milk, oil, and vanilla to the egg and mix. In a separate bowl, combine the flour, sugar, salt, and, for the plain-flour option only, baking powder.
4. Add the wet ingredients to the dry ingredients and stir just until combined. Do not keep mixing once no large patches of dry flour remain.
5. Divide the batter among 18 muffin holes, filling each about two-thirds to three-quarters full.
6. Bake for 20–25 minutes, beginning to check at about 18 minutes. Check the dark nonstick tin first, since it may brown faster. The muffins are ready when they spring back lightly and a skewer comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs.
7. If the tins are on two shelves, swap their positions and turn them front to back halfway through, working quickly. Cool the muffins in the tins for several minutes, then transfer them to a rack.
