Include the https:// portion unless your teacher or the school’s style sheet says otherwise. It makes the link complete and clickable in many word processors. MLA entries are alphabetized by the first meaningful element, and each entry uses a hanging indent.

“Weekly Sky Notes.” Sky Notes Online, 15 Jan. 2024, https://skynotes.example/weekly.

In a word processor:
1. Select all bibliography entries.
2. Open Paragraph settings.
3. Set “Special” or “Indentation” to “Hanging.”
4. Set the hanging amount to 0.5 inch, or 1.27 cm.
5. Set line spacing to double if the rest of the handout is double-spaced.
6. Do not add extra blank space between entries.
7. Keep the entries aligned to the left margin except for the hanging lines.

I’m using Google Docs. I can make the hanging indent, but the URLs sometimes turn blue and wrap awkwardly. What exact settings should I use, and is it okay to shorten the links?

[assistantassistant]
In Google Docs, use these settings:

1. Highlight only the bibliography entries, including all wrapped lines.
2. Choose Format > Align and indent > Indentation options.
3. Set Left to 0.
4. Set Special indent to Hanging.
5. Set the hanging value to 0.5.
6. Choose Apply.
7. Use Format > Line & paragraph spacing > Double.
8. If needed, choose Custom spacing and set Before paragraph and After paragraph to 0.

For the links, keep the full URLs supplied in your notes. Do not replace them with a shortened service link, because the shortened address may hide the destination and may not meet the assignment’s source-documentation requirements. Blue, underlined links are acceptable unless the handout has a specific visual rule. To remove automatic hyperlink formatting without deleting the text, click the link and choose the unlink option. The URL will remain visible as plain text.

To reduce awkward wrapping, you can widen the text area only if that matches the handout layout. Avoid inserting manual line breaks inside an entry; Google Docs will wrap the line automatically while preserving the hanging indent.
