I’m measuring the baking powder and soda by volume because my scale isn’t reliable at low weights. Can you translate those, and explain how to divide the batter evenly without deflating it?

[assistant]
For the leaveners:

- 10.5 g baking powder is approximately 2 5/8 teaspoons. Measure 2 1/2 teaspoons plus 1/8 teaspoon, or use a level 2 1/2 teaspoons with a small extra pinch.
- 2.1 g baking soda is approximately 7/16 teaspoon. Measure 1/2 teaspoon, then remove a small pinch; alternatively, use a scant 1/2 teaspoon.

Spoon each leavener into the measuring spoon and level it with a straight edge. Avoid packing the powder or soda.

To divide the batter evenly without overmixing:

1. Place an empty bowl on the scale and tare it before mixing, or record the bowl’s empty weight.
2. After the batter is finished, weigh the full mixing bowl and subtract the bowl weight to determine the batter weight.
3. Tare each prepared cake pan, or weigh each empty pan and record its weight.
4. Add half the batter’s net weight to the first pan, then repeat for the second. If you cannot tare the pans, use the recorded pan weight and stop when pan-plus-batter reaches the target.
5. Transfer with a flexible spatula, using a few deliberate folds rather than scraping aggressively. Divide promptly because the chemical leaveners begin reacting once liquid is present.
6. Level each pan gently by nudging the batter outward with an offset spatula. A brief, light tap on the counter is fine, but do not repeatedly bang the pans.

For storage until tomorrow afternoon:

1. Cool the layers in their pans for 10–15 minutes.
2. Turn them out onto a rack, remove the parchment, and cool completely.
3. Wrap each layer tightly in plastic wrap, then place them in an airtight container or a sealed bag.
4. Keep them at cool room temperature overnight if your kitchen is below about 22°C. Refrigerate if the room is warmer, but wrap them well to prevent drying and odor absorption.
5. Assemble and frost tomorrow. If refrigerated, let the wrapped layers sit at room temperature for 30–60 minutes before handling; chilled layers are less fragile, though.

Avoid storing an unfrosted cake uncovered in the refrigerator. The cut surfaces dry quickly, and the refrigerator can impart odors.
