[assistant]
The entries are normally alternative operating modes, not values that are all delivered simultaneously. A USB-PD charger advertises a set of voltage-and-current “power data objects,” and the phone requests one compatible mode. In simplified form, the charger might offer:

Charger B:
- 5V at up to 3A: 15W
- 9V at up to 2.77A: about 24.9W
- 12V at up to 2.08A: about 25W

The charger does not provide 5V, 9V, and 12V to one phone at once. The requested mode determines the output voltage, and the current varies up to that mode’s limit.

The non-round current values are likely the result of setting a power ceiling first and dividing by voltage:
- 25W ÷ 9V = 2.777…A
- 25W ÷ 12V = 2.083…A

The label rounds those values to 2.77A and 2.08A. This keeps the advertised power close to 25W at both voltages. Charger A follows the same pattern at 18W:
- 18W ÷ 9V = 2A
- 18W ÷ 12V = 1.5A
