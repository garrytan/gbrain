[user]
The label says: “Water when the top inch of soil is dry. Keep evenly moist during active growth. Reduce watering in winter. Avoid standing water.” It’s in a 12 cm pot with one drainage hole. It sits about a meter from a bright window, but direct sun only reaches it briefly in the morning.

[assistant]
That wording suggests checking the soil rather than watering on fixed calendar days. For a 12 cm pot with one drainage hole, start with this routine:

- Check the top inch, about 2.5 cm, every three or four days.
- During active growth, water when that layer feels dry, usually about once every 7–10 days indoors.
- Pour slowly until a little water comes from the drainage hole.
- Empty the saucer after about 10 minutes.
- In winter, check weekly, but water only when the top inch is dry; the interval may become roughly 10–14 days.

The exact amount depends on the soil and pot shape, so drainage is a better stopping signal than a fixed volume.

[user]
I’m not sure how to judge “top inch.” I have a wooden skewer and a small measuring cup. What should I do?

[assistant]
Use the skewer as a depth check:

1. Push it straight into the soil about 2.5 cm from the surface, avoiding the stem.
2. Leave it in for a few seconds and pull it out.
3. If it comes out with damp soil sticking to it or looks dark and cool, wait.
4. If it comes out mostly clean and the soil at that depth feels dry, water.

You can use the measuring cup to learn the pot’s approximate amount. On the first watering, pour slowly in 100 ml portions, pausing between portions. Stop when a small amount drains below. Record the total, but do not treat that number as permanent if the soil dries at a different rate.

[user]
Could I just water every Sunday so I don’t forget? I’d rather have a regular reminder than keep checking randomly.

[assistant]
A weekly reminder is fine as a prompt, but make the actual watering conditional. Each Sunday, do the skewer test first:

- If the top 2.5 cm is dry, water slowly until a little drains out.
- If it is still damp, skip watering and test again in two or three days.
- If the surface is dry but the skewer shows moisture below, wait another day or two.
- If water runs straight through immediately, the soil may be very dry; add smaller amounts, pause, and repeat.

This gives you a predictable check without forcing water into soil that is still wet.

How much should I put in the measuring cup? The pot is roughly 12 cm wide and maybe 10 cm tall, but it narrows slightly toward the bottom.

[assistant]
For that size pot, begin with about 250–350 ml total, poured gradually. The useful endpoint is a little drainage, not the exact number. Try this:

- Pour 100 ml and wait 30 seconds.
- Pour another 100 ml.
- Check underneath or in the saucer.
- Add 50 ml at a time until a small amount exits the hole.
- Leave it for 10 minutes, then empty the saucer.

If drainage starts before 250 ml, stop there. If the soil is very dry and water runs down the sides, wait a few minutes and give another small portion rather than pouring the whole amount quickly.

[user]
What if the surface looks dry tomorrow but the plant still looks fine? I’m worried I’ll either overwater or wait too long.

[assistant]
A dry-looking surface by itself is not enough to trigger watering. The top layer can look pale while moisture remains below it. Use the skewer at the labeled depth, and also lift the pot if convenient:

- Skewer damp at 2.5 cm: wait, even if the surface looks dry.
- Skewer dry and pot still feels fairly heavy: check again the next day.
- Skewer dry and pot feels noticeably lighter: water slowly.
- Leaves slightly droop but soil is damp: do not add water; inspect the light, temperature, and drainage instead.
- Leaves droop and the soil is dry at 2.5 cm: water as usual.

Avoid trying to compensate with small daily sips, since that can keep the upper soil unevenly moist.
