Use the heading “Works Cited,” centered at the top. Do not bold, italicize, underline, or put it in quotation marks unless your teacher has a different format requirement.

Set the page as follows:

- Use a readable 12-point font, unless the assignment specifies another font.
- Use one-inch margins.
- Double-space every line, including blank space between entries; do not add extra spacing between entries.
- Apply a hanging indent of 0.5 inch to each entry.
- Alphabetize by the first element of each entry.
- Continue the page number sequence from the rest of the handout, usually in the upper-right header.

Here is the content in order:

[user]
I’m using a plain-text editor first. How can I represent the hanging indents and italics there without accidentally changing the citation text?

[assistant]
Plain text cannot preserve true italics or indentation settings, so treat it as a content draft and apply the visual formatting later in a word processor.

For plain-text notes:

- Keep the citation wording and punctuation exactly as written.
- Use a blank line between entries only if that helps you read the draft; remove those blank lines when applying MLA double spacing.
- Do not insert tabs or spaces at the beginning of continuation lines unless you are making a temporary visual mock-up.
- Mark italicized titles with a note such as `[italics]` rather than adding underscores or asterisks that might remain accidentally.
- Keep URLs as ordinary text.
- Save a clean copy before transferring the entries.

For example, you could write:

Ortiz, Lena. [italics: The Practical Garden: Small-Space Planting]. Green Field Press, 2021.

When you move this into a formatted document, delete `[italics: ...]`, select the title, and apply italics. The same applies to *Journal of Urban Horticulture*, *Home Study Garden*, and *Video Example*.
