My teacher says to use hanging indents and double spacing. I’m typing in Google Docs. What exact steps should I follow?

[assistant]
In Google Docs:

1. Put all five entries in one document or bibliography section.
2. Select the entries.
3. Open **Format** → **Line & paragraph spacing** → **Double**.
4. With the text still selected, open **Format** → **Align & indent** → **Indentation options**.
5. In the dialog box, set **Special indent** to **Hanging**.
6. Set the hanging indent to **0.5** inches, then select **Apply**.
7. Use left alignment, not justified alignment.
8. Make sure there is no extra blank line between entries. Double spacing supplies the required space.
9. If the bibliography has a heading, center the heading without bold, italics, quotation marks, or underlining unless your teacher specifies otherwise.

You can verify the result by checking that the first line of each entry begins at the left margin and any wrapped lines begin 0.5 inches to the right.

Should I keep the URLs as clickable links, and do I need to add access dates?

[assistant]
Usually, keep the URLs in the entries, but follow your teacher’s preference about clickable formatting. In Google Docs, a blue underlined link is generally acceptable unless the assignment asks for plain text. The important part is that the URL itself is complete and readable.

For access dates, MLA 9 does not require them for every online source. Add one when your teacher requests it, when the page has no publication or update date, or when the content is likely to change. If you add dates, use this form:

```text
Accessed 30 Sept. 2026.
```

Place the access date at the end of the relevant entry, before the final period if the entry already ends with a URL or other element. For example:

```text
“Recycling at School.” *City Education Office*, 7 Sept. 2021, www.example.edu/recycling. Accessed 30 Sept. 2026.
```

Because access dates describe when you viewed the source, use the actual date you accessed it rather than copying the current date automatically.
