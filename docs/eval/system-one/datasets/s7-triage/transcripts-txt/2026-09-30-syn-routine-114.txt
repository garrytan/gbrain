The first charger can provide up to about 20W at 9V, while the second can provide up to about 18W at 9V:

- Charger A: 9V × 2.22A ≈ 20W
- Charger B: 9V × 2A = 18W

At 5V, both are rated for 15W, and at 12V both are rated for 18W. So the difference is small and only matters if your phone requests a 9V charging mode and can use more than 18W. Otherwise, they may charge at the same speed.

[user]
The labels also have small text. Charger A says “USB PD 3.0 PPS,” while Charger B says “QC 3.0” and “USB output.” My phone is a fairly recent USB-C phone, but I don’t know which charging standard it supports. Does that change the comparison?

[assistant]
Yes. The protocol markings may matter more than the maximum wattage.

USB Power Delivery (PD) is the common standard used by many recent USB-C phones. PPS, or Programmable Power Supply, is an optional PD feature that lets the phone request voltage in smaller steps rather than selecting only fixed levels such as 5V, 9V, or 12V. If your phone supports PD PPS, Charger A may charge it more efficiently or at its advertised fast-charge rate.

“QC 3.0” refers to Quick Charge. Some phones support it, but USB-C phones do not automatically support every QC implementation. Charger B might still charge your phone normally, but it may fall back to a slower 5V mode if the phone does not recognize QC.

How can I check compatibility without relying only on the marketing text? I have the charger boxes, but neither box lists a phone model. I can also inspect the phone settings and use a cable tester, though the tester only shows voltage and current.

[assistant]
Use several checks:

1. Look in the phone’s manual, support page, or charging settings for terms such as “USB PD,” “PD,” “PPS,” or “Quick Charge.” A setting labeled “fast charging” is not specific enough by itself.
2. Read the complete charger output table. For Charger A, look for PD fixed outputs and a PPS range, such as “PPS: 3.3–11V⎓1.8A.” For Charger B, check whether it lists USB-PD at all, rather than only QC.
3. Use a known-good USB-C-to-USB-C cable. A USB-A-to-USB-C cable may limit which protocols are available.
4. Measure while the phone is actively charging, with the screen on and battery below roughly 70%. A cable tester showing around 9V suggests a negotiated higher-voltage mode, but it does not prove that the phone is using PD PPS.
5. Compare charging time under the same conditions. Avoid drawing conclusions from a brief current reading because charging power changes with battery level and temperature.
