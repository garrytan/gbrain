That wording suggests checking the soil rather than watering on fixed weekdays. A practical starting routine would be:

- Check the top inch every 3–4 days.
- If it feels dry at that depth, water slowly until a little drains from the bottom.
- Empty the saucer after about 10 minutes.
- If the top inch is still damp, wait and check again in 2 days.
- In winter, begin by checking every 5–7 days instead.

For a 6-inch pot, the amount may be roughly 2–3 cups, but the drainage result matters more than the exact volume. The first few waterings can help you estimate how much produces a small amount of runoff without leaving the pot standing in water.

[user]
I’d prefer something more calendar-like so I don’t forget. Could I water every Sunday and Thursday?

[assistant]
You can use Sunday and Thursday as check-in days, but I would not automatically water on both days. Use this routine:

Sunday:
1. Push a finger or wooden stick about 1 inch into the soil.
2. If that section is dry, water thoroughly.
3. If it is damp, skip watering.

Thursday:
1. Check the same depth.
2. Water only if it is dry.
3. If it is still damp, skip it and check again Sunday.

This preserves the label’s “when dry” instruction while giving you fixed reminders. In a warm, bright room, twice-weekly checks may be useful, but the plant may actually need water only once every 7–14 days depending on soil and airflow.

[user]
How much water should I pour each time? I have a 1-liter measuring jug.

[assistant]
Start with about 500–700 milliliters, poured slowly around the soil surface. Watch for drainage from the holes. If no water appears after that amount, add another 100–150 milliliters and wait briefly. Once a small amount drains, stop pouring and let the pot finish draining.

The useful target is thorough wetting with modest runoff, not a fixed number. If the soil has become very dry and pulls away from the pot edge, pour half the amount first, wait 5–10 minutes, then apply the rest. This helps prevent water from running straight through gaps without moistening the root ball.

[user]
Would it be easier to use a moisture meter? I saw inexpensive ones online, but I don’t know whether the reading is reliable.

[assistant]
A basic moisture meter can be a convenient extra check, but it should not replace the finger test entirely. Inexpensive meters can be inconsistent because they measure electrical conductivity, which is affected by fertilizer, soil composition, and contact with the potting mix.

If you use one:

- Insert it about halfway between the stem and the pot edge.
- Push it roughly 2–3 inches deep, avoiding the drainage hole.
- Take readings in two spots if the first reading seems surprising.
- Wipe the probe clean after use.
- Treat the reading as a trend rather than an exact percentage.

You can calibrate your routine by comparing the meter with the top-inch test for several checks. If the top inch is dry and the deeper soil is also reading dry, water. If the surface is dry but deeper soil is clearly moist, wait.

The potting mix is a dense-looking peat mix, and the label on the bag says it holds moisture. Does that change the Sunday-and-Thursday plan?

[assistant]
Yes, a moisture-holding peat mix may dry more slowly, so the plant might need fewer actual waterings. Keep Sunday and Thursday as inspection days, but avoid watering simply because it is one of those days.

For this setup, try:

- Sunday: check the top inch and lift the pot if you can.
- Thursday: check the top inch again only if Sunday was damp, or check the pot’s weight if that is easier.
- Water when the top inch is dry and the pot feels noticeably lighter.
- After watering, let all excess drain before returning it to the saucer.

If the mix stays damp for more than 10–14 days, check that the drainage holes are open and that the pot is not sitting in collected water. Dense mix plus frequent watering is more likely to cause problems than missing one scheduled watering.

[user]
What does “keep evenly moist” mean in practice? Does the soil need to be damp all the time?

[assistantassistant]
It usually means the root-zone moisture should change gradually rather than cycling between saturated and bone-dry conditions. It does not mean the soil surface must always feel wet.

For your label, interpret it this way:

- Allow the top inch to dry.
- Do not wait until the entire pot is dry and lightweight.
- Water thoroughly enough to moisten the full mix.
- Let excess water drain away.
- Do not leave the bottom of the pot submerged in water.

A damp-but-not-soggy mix may feel cool and slightly cohesive when squeezed, while saturated mix can feel heavy, shiny, or muddy. If the top inch dries quickly but the lower part remains wet, increase the interval and consider improving airflow rather than adding more water.

[user]
Can I use the same schedule in winter if the room stays near 70°F?

[assistant]
Use the same check-in days, but expect to water less often. A steady room temperature does not guarantee the same drying rate because winter usually brings less direct light and sometimes lower plant activity.

A reasonable winter version is:

- Check on Sunday.
- Check again on Thursday only if the pot still feels fairly light or the surface has dried.
- Water when the top inch is dry, rather than automatically every week.
- Start with a smaller expected amount, perhaps 400–600 milliliters, and still water until a little drains.
- Recheck the soil after 3–4 days if the mix was very dry.

If the plant is near a heater, the air may dry faster, but the mix can still stay wet if light is limited. Use the soil and pot weight as the deciding indicators.

[user]
I sometimes travel for five or six days. If I water thoroughly before leaving, is that enough?

[assistant]
For a five- or six-day absence, it will often be enough if the soil is close to the label’s “water when the top inch is dry” point before you leave. Do not add extra water just because you will be away.

Before leaving:

1. Check the top inch.
2. If it is dry, water slowly until a little drains.
3. Empty the saucer.
4. Move the pot slightly farther from strong window heat if the room is warm.
5. Leave it in bright, indirect light, not direct afternoon sun.

If the top inch is still damp, skip the watering. A plant is generally safer going several days in slightly damp soil than sitting in a saturated pot. When you return, check the soil before watering.

At the end of two weeks

- Keep the notes simple: date, soil condition, pot weight, amount added, and whether drainage occurred.

One detail: the plant is in a decorative outer pot, and I’m not sure whether water collects underneath the plastic pot. How should I check?

[assistant]
Lift the plastic nursery pot out of the decorative pot before watering if possible. Inspect the bottom of the outer pot for any collected water, and pour that water away.

After watering:

1. Place the plastic pot in a sink, tray, or bathtub.
2. Water until a small amount exits the drainage holes.
3. Let it drain for 10–15 minutes.
4. Check the bottom of the decorative pot.
5. Return the plastic pot only when the outer pot is empty.

If the plastic pot must remain inside, water more cautiously and check the outer pot after 10 minutes. Do not allow the nursery pot to sit in pooled water, even if the surface soil looks dry.

What if the meter says “wet,” but the top inch feels dry? Which should I trust?

[assistant]
For this plant, trust the soil profile rather than either reading by itself. A dry top inch with wet soil deeper down usually means the plant does not need water yet, especially in a moisture-holding peat mix.

Check these points:

- Insert the meter in two locations, avoiding the pot wall and drainage hole.
- Feel the soil at 1 inch and, if practical, at about 2–3 inches.
- Lift the pot and compare its weight with the weight after watering.
- Look for drainage problems or compacted areas.

If the top inch is dry, the lower soil is moist, and the pot is still heavy, wait 2–3 days. If the top inch is dry, the deeper soil is only slightly moist, and the pot feels light, water slowly. If the meter remains “wet” for more than 10–14 days while the soil smells sour or drainage is poor, stop relying on it and inspect the pot and mix.
