[assistant]
Sure. Start with the filesystem summary, then check the largest top-level directories:

```sh
df -h "$HOME"
du -xhd1 "$HOME" 2>/dev/null | sort -h
```

`df` shows space available to the filesystem, while `du` estimates what files under your home directory are using. Paste both outputs, and avoid deleting anything until the large directories are identified.

There are several likely cleanup targets, but the safe action depends on what each directory contains. Get file counts, ages, and a little detail from the large areas:

```sh

find "$HOME/.containers" -xdev -type f -printf '%s %TY-%Tm-%Td %p\n' 2>/dev/null |
  sort -n | tail -20
```
