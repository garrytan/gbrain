# Managed sync reliability contract (#6317) and the two-consumer `preparing` wedge (#6278 follow-up)

Thread: GBRA-61. Issues: #6317 (new), #6278 (comments of 2026-10-08 15:15Z and 15:25Z). Stacks on #6298 (GBRA-59, PR 1 of #6278) which stacks on #6279 (GBRA-45). Target: PR 3 of the #6278 family, `garrytan/gbrain`, version stamped at the merge slot (master + 1 PATCH).

## 0. Why now

Garry's production brain (the #6278 deployment: 0.60.110.0, Postgres via Supavisor transaction pooler, 13,627-entry managed worktree, bulk lanes 6) has not moved sync data since 2026-09-20. Eighteen days, seven upgrades, every health signal green. #6298 contains the wedge (a budget cuts a stuck preparation at 120 s, the root is freed by the 600 s ceiling at the latest, and a member cut twice is held so the catch-up keeps going). This plan is what #6298 does not do: find and remove the cause of this deployment's wedge, make a second wedge of any class impossible to miss, and make the system refuse to call itself healthy while data does not move.

## 1. What is established (evidence, not hypothesis)

From the reporter's live captures on 0.60.110.0 (#6278, 15:15Z and 15:25Z comments):

- E1. 16 `managed_sync_import` requests of one bulk group (manifest 529–544) sat in `state='running'`, `claim_phase.phase='preparing'` for 25+ minutes while the lease renewed every 10 s. `pg_stat_activity`: no statement older than ~3 s, no lock wait over ~1 s, no `idle in transaction` over ~5 s. `statement_timeout = 10min` on both the pooler and the direct URL. Owner process at 5–20 % CPU, no child processes, no git locks. **So this deployment's wedge is a never-settling JS await with nothing in flight at the database.** The Phase 0 class #6298 proved (a relation lock wait a transaction-mode pooler hides) is real but is not this deployment's class.
- E2. `prepareManagedSyncMutation` on the same 16 rows from a scratch process (fresh engine, pool 10, same host, same DB) completes in 2.6–3.4 s each, 15 s for all 16 four at a time. All 16 intents are `lineEndingOnly: true`. **Nothing in the data or in prepare's own logic hangs.**
- E3. Every wedge had two persistence consumers alive on the host: the `serve`-resident consumer and the consumer inside the `gbrain sync` CLI (lanes `configured: 6, effective: 6`, so the CLI owned the `laneRun`). The group was claimed by `serve` 7 s after it booted, 77 s before any sync CLI existed. With the CLI killed and `serve` alone, the same group reached `publishing` within 22 s and committed. **Observed once: every wedge had at least two consumer-bearing processes on the host, and the one single-consumer attempt published.** Up to three processes hosted consumers (`src/core/persistence/service.ts:160-163`: any process that waits on a write starts one, and the jobs worker waited on 45 adoption writes), and the lone-consumer success was a process 11 minutes old, so "lone process" and "fresh process" are not yet separated (Phase 0 arms ii and iii).
- E4. `fence_repair` / chronicle `owner_unavailable` for weeks: job workers launched with `HOME=/root` (non-persistent) and `GBRAIN_HOME` unset minted a new `host.json` identity after every container restart and saw the binding as another host's. Fixed on their side with `GBRAIN_HOME=/data`; nothing in gbrain pointed at it.
- E5. `runDrain`'s stall probe could not fire: fingerprint included `claim_expires_at` (owner-refreshed) and `drain_stalled` required `!claimable_here` (false on the same host). The progress meter printed an ETA from the last good window. Seven upgrades passed process-level acceptance (`/health` ok, PID alive, `sync_running: true`).

What #6298 (head 471094d3) already ships against #6317, verified by reading its diff:

- #6317 item 1 (phase ceiling by phase age): yes. `preparation-budget.ts` races every preparation against its budget ("the budget wins even when the preparer ignores its signal"); `preparation_ceiling_ms` (600 s) frees the root from an abandoned preparation; `max_preparation_attempts` (2) finishes the request `failed`/`preparation_stalled`.
- #6317 item 2 (drain fingerprint, `claimable_here`): yes for the fingerprint ("Lease columns (updated_at, claim_expires_at) are deliberately absent: a renewal is not progress") and `preparation_overdue` → `preparation_abandoned` when the owner pid is this process. The `!claimable_here` condition still gates the generic `drain_stalled`; this plan removes it (section 3, B2).
- #6317 item 4 (observability): partly. `claim_phase` stamps carry phase, step (~27 await boundaries), `waitingOn` (`git|fs|db|pool|unknown`) and the owner process (kind, pid, version); writer status blockers carry `operation`, `intent_kind`, `preparation_attempts`, `claim.stall`; a live `stalled <N>s on <step>` progress line prints during a pass. Missing (GBRA-59 confirmed 2026-10-08): the pool gauge is not exposed (`waiting_on: 'pool'` only when instrumented), no shipped stack dump (Phase 0 used a debug-only SIGUSR2 installer, never shipped), no "consumers alive on this host".
- Item 5: GBRA-59's PR 2 adds the `owner_unavailable` reasons matrix (`host_mismatch` with both ids' prefixes); the `host_identity_split` doctor check and `GBRAIN_HOME` propagation for job workers were deferred there "until evidence" and are now this plan's I1 (agreed with GBRA-59).
- Items 3 and 6: not covered anywhere.

What #6298 does to this deployment's wedge, per GBRA-59: release at 120 s → root stays blocked while the zombie await lives, up to `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms` (600 s from claim start) → at the ceiling the root is freed, attempts pinned at the limit, the next claim finishes the request `preparation_stalled`, the sync holds that file and continues; the breaker stops the run `preparation_systemic` after 5 consecutive stalls with no commit. So each wedge costs ≤ 10 minutes and one healthy file held. Containment, not a fix.

## 3. Build (one PR, stacked on #6298; lanes are parallel subagents, integrator merges)

### Lane A: remove the cause

- A1. Fix the named await from Phase 0 with a forced-probe regression test (fails on #6298's head, passes with the fix; the repo's flake rule) in the two-process shape (serve consumer + CLI consumer against one Postgres, lanes > 1): with B1 on, the group publishes and only the `serve` pid appears in its members' `claim_phase.owner`; with `--no-delegate`, the group publishes within the window whichever process claims it.
- A2. If the cause is the pool: the preparation path takes its connections through the long-hold budget (`pool-budget.ts`) like publication does, so a wave of four prepares can never hold every ordinary connection; a prepare that would need the last connection waits with `waitingOn: 'pool'` and the budget cuts it.
- A3. If the cause is cross-process state: the fix is B1 (one consumer) plus the narrowest guard Phase 0 names. (The plan's earlier `laneOf` refusal is dropped: `sync-lanes.ts:75-79` already returns null for a row whose run is not open in this process, so a foreign-run group already takes the FIFO path.)

### Lane B: one consumer per host, and a stall that can fire

- B1. **One full consumer per host: the first live resident consumer wins, as a preference.** Split into four parts; B1a, B1b and B1d ship in every case; the default of the brain-wide switch `persistence.single_consumer` is set by Phase 0's preregistered reading (on only when arm (i) wedges and arm (ii) does not; otherwise shipped off, because funnelling every write through an owner that wedges on its own would turn one stuck write into a host-wide stall); B1c exists only under D1 (a) and waits for Phase 0 arms (i) and (ii) (see §2). Scope of the rule: resident kinds (`sync`, `jobs`, `autopilot`, `mcp`) defer to the first live full consumer; a `serve` always starts full; short-lived foreground commands (`put`, `import`, `dream`, `cycle`, `sources`, `cli`) keep today's own consumer for the life of their write, because `awaitWrite`'s wake is in-process only (`pr6298:service.ts:195` `consumer.wake(true)`; no LISTEN/NOTIFY) and the owner's idle backoff reaches 5 s (`pr6298:consumer.ts:193,210`), so a waiter-only `gbrain put` would often return `writer_pending`; a cross-process wake (`pg_notify` on admission) is a TODO, not this PR.
  - B1a (probe). The rule lives in `startPersistenceConsumer` (`src/core/persistence/service.ts:80`), so it covers every process that waits on a write (`awaitWrite`, `service.ts:160-163`: the sync CLI, the jobs worker's adoption writes, `gbrain put`), not only `drainManagedSync`. The probe is the database, never a socket: a live row for this host in the B3 `persistence_consumers` table of a resident kind (`serve`, `sync`, `jobs`, `autopilot`, `mcp`), mode `full`, that is not this process (renewed within 30 s; leases are 30 s, `consumer.ts` `claimLeaseMs ?? 30_000`, renewed every 10 s, `claim-lease.ts`; "lapsed" below means 60 s without renewal), not `restart_required` (the owner reached #6298's zombie cap, `consumer.ts` `DEFAULT_ZOMBIE_CAP`/`restartRequired()`) and not `wedged` (the row carries, from the owner's own `status()`, its zombie count and the age of its oldest abandoned root barrier, because #6298 releases a request at its budget and keeps only an in-process root barrier until the ceiling (`pr6298:consumer.ts:678-694,735-745`), so an "oldest running claim" would miss exactly the abandoned preparation; `wedged` = `restart_required` or any root barrier older than `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms`, and a wedged owner stops suppressing), whose binding is `state='active'`. A `serve` always starts full (so a `serve` started beside a running CLI makes two full consumers until that run ends, listed by doctor meanwhile); every other resident kind defers to the first live full row. The persistence IPC socket is not used: `src/mcp/resolve-ipc-binding.ts:103-113` opens it only when `owner.engine === 'pglite'`. A probe the database cannot answer falls back to today's behaviour with one log line. Residual: two processes that start within one renewal of each other can both read no live row and both start full, and nothing bounds that overlap by time (the heartbeat is evidence of a process, not a fenced role; there is no demotion of a consumer that started full). Reconciliation is minimal and explicit: only a consumer that was *promoted* from waiter-only drains back (finishes its in-flight tasks, claims nothing new, returns to `waiter_only`) once the elected owner's row is live and healthy again for three consecutive ticks, so a promotion storm after a heartbeat outage converges; two consumers that both started full stay full until one exits, which doctor names. T1b races two starters and asserts persistent overlap is reported, and tests owner recovery after a promotion. This is a preference the plan states as such, not a fenced invariant; a fenced consumer role is deferred to the thin-client parity follow-up. Process identity in the stamp and the row is `{kind, pid, nonce, pid_ns}` (a random per-process nonce and the pid-namespace inode from `/proc/self/ns/pid` where readable), because containers sharing one `GBRAIN_HOME` share a host id and reuse pids (`pr6298:claim-phase.ts:93-96` records only kind, pid, version today); `owner_pid === process.pid` checks (`pr6298:sync-drain.ts:329`) compare the nonce too.
  - B1b (waiter-only seam). When the probe finds a live full resident consumer that is not this process, `startPersistenceConsumer` returns a `WaiterOnlyConsumer` with the consumer's whole external surface (`wake()` no-op, settlement listeners through `settledWaiters`, `holds()` false, `foregroundCompletions()` 0, `status()` reporting `waiter_only` with the owner's pid, `stop()` no-op; the existing call sites need no change) that claims nothing and writes no heartbeat row. `awaitWrite`'s own timeout is unchanged: a waiter-only process returns `writer_pending` to its caller exactly as a process whose consumer is busy does today, and never waits longer than the owner's tick plus one lease. `startPersistenceConsumer` stays synchronous (`pr6298:service.ts:88`, called synchronously at `:108` and `:182`): the consumer starts in a `probing` mode that claims nothing and runs the heartbeat probe on its first tick, then settles to `full` or `waiter_only`; a waiter-only consumer re-probes on every tick (one indexed read on the consumers table) and promotes when its owner's row is lapsed, `wedged`, not `full`, or gone, so promotion follows the owner's progress, not only its liveness (an owner at the zombie cap, `DEFAULT_ZOMBIE_CAP = 1`, `pr6298:consumer.ts:42,483`, stops claiming while its heartbeat keeps going; without the per-tick re-probe no process on the host would consume). The probe and the heartbeat are single-flight with a cancellable deadline (the consumer's `phaseMs`), never a queue of timers; `stop()` cancels and awaits the in-flight probe or promotion before the engine closes, and a probe result that arrives after `stop()` is dropped; a probe that cannot answer within its deadline leaves the consumer in `probing` for that tick and logs once after three misses. A waiter-only process's `writer_pending` envelope names the owner (`kind`, pid, `claim.stall.step`) and puts `gbrain sources writer status --source <id> --json` in `fix.verify`, so an agent whose `gbrain put` waits on a wedged owner is told who holds it instead of polling a receipt that will not settle. `promote()` turns it into a full consumer in place (same `services` entry): called when the owner's heartbeat lapses 60 s while a waiter is pending (one log line, `[persistence] owner_lapsed promoted_to_consumer`) and by the skew fallback in B1d. A live but wedged owner keeps renewing; its row's `claim_past_ceiling` flag (B1a) makes it stop suppressing after the 600 s ceiling, and before that B2's `drain_stalled` (naming the owner pid) and B4/I2 are the exits, with the owner's own #6298 budget as the containment (T2). A `serve` is always a full consumer; a CLI that took its own consumer before a `serve` started is not demoted (its run ends; doctor lists both meanwhile). PGLite is unchanged (its single-writer lock already allows one process). An owner on another host never suppresses (ownership is per host, `persistence_worktrees.owner_host_id`).
  - B1c (lane policy over the resolve IPC). Managed sync on top of that: the CLI runs as a pure driver (admits groups, saves the cursor, waits on receipts, prints progress) and sends the lane policy to the owner over the resolve IPC socket every `serve` already opens (`resolve-ipc-binding.ts:100-102`, the transport of the `sync_start|sync_status|sync_abort` family, shared-secret checked), as two new kinds `lanes_open {worktree_id, run, lanes}` and `lanes_close {run}` (new members of the `IpcRequest` union and handler type in `resolve-ipc.ts`; accepted only from a verified-secret caller on a managed brain for a worktree this host owns). This is not "lane logic unchanged": the CLI's admission reads lane state from its own process (`pr6298:sync-run.ts:806` `laneApplyMsPerMember` for group sizing, `:873` `lanePolicy().effective` for admission depth, `:977` the lanes progress line, `:846` `laneRunOf` calling `openLanes` locally), and step-down happens where the group publishes (`pr6298:group-publish.ts:416`), which under (a) is the owner. So under (a) the CLI must not call `openLanes` locally; it reads the effective lane count and apply timing back from the owner in each `lanes_renew` reply and sizes groups from that. Three kinds, not two: `lanes_open` (idempotent; the owner calls `openLanes(worktreeId, run, lanes, coordinationPath)`, `pr6298:sync-lanes.ts:45`, once per run), `lanes_renew {run}` every 10 s (the CLI heartbeat; it never calls `openLanes`, which replaces the whole `LaneState` including the `claimed`/`begun` sets, task counts and step-down, `pr6298:sync-lanes.ts:44-47`), and `lanes_close {run}` (the owner marks the run `closing`, so a late renewal is rejected, awaits its own `closeLaneRun`, `pr6298:sync-lanes.ts:54-60`, and only then runs `cancelOrphanedLaneRows`; the CLI's `finally` waits for the owner's reply before it cancels anything, so the owner's in-flight claims are never cancelled under it). Open and renew are serialized per worktree and run in the owner. The handshake precedes the CLI's first *admission*, not merely its first `awaitWrite`, because admission already exposes rows and wakes consumers (`pr6298:sync-run.ts:914-934`). The owner closes a lane run whose renewals stop for 60 s. The three kinds are registered inside the `GBRAIN_SERVE_SYNC_IPC` block (`resolve-ipc-binding.ts:129-131`) so the kill switch disables them with the sync family and B1d's fallback holds. A promoted CLI (B1b) whose run is open in the owner never becomes a second lane runner: `laneRoots()` in a process that handed a run off returns nothing for that run. New client code: a Postgres branch of the delegation ladder (today `sync-delegate.ts:160` gates on `probeLivePgliteHolder`).
  - B1d (skew and escape hatch). The `lanes_open` handshake precedes the CLI's first `awaitWrite`. An owner that cannot take the hand-off makes the CLI print one actionable warning (`[sync] the running gbrain serve (pid N) predates lane hand-off; this run uses its own consumer; restart that serve on this gbrain version to remove the second consumer`), set the process-level own-consumer flag and `promote()` any waiter-only consumer already made, so that run behaves as today. The real signals of an older owner are what the existing client already classifies, not a stipulated `unsupported_kind`: `unknown_kind:<kind>` from the base server, a missing protocol echo (`stale_serve`), `IPC_UNAVAILABLE`, a missing socket or `GBRAIN_SERVE_SYNC_IPC=0` (`pr6298:src/core/context/resolve-ipc.ts:683-693,860-881`, `resolve-ipc-binding.ts:129-131`); T1c tests a real older `serve` build, not a stub. Mixed versions the other way: an older `serve` writes no heartbeat row, so a new CLI beside it starts full (two full consumers until the serve is upgraded); the CLI's start line says so when the binding owner is active but no row exists (`owner row missing: older serve or no serve; running own consumer`), and the behaviour notice states it. Flags: the existing `gbrain sync --no-delegate` / `GBRAIN_SYNC_NO_DELEGATE=1` (`src/commands/sync/args.ts:107`) extend to Postgres and mean "keep your own consumer" on both engines (one flag, help text rewritten in user terms; no new `--own-consumer`); the brain-wide off switch is `persistence.single_consumer` (default on) with env mirror `GBRAIN_SINGLE_CONSUMER=0`, following the `persistence.preparation_deadlines` / `GBRAIN_PREPARATION_DEADLINES` pattern, so a supervisor running three processes restores today's behaviour in one place. When no live full resident consumer exists: today's behaviour. Decision D1 below covers the alternatives, including (a′) handing the whole drain to the serve through the existing `sync_start` kind.
- B2. `runDrain` notices a same-host stall it cannot fire on today (the gate at `pr6298:sync-drain.ts:325` `!print.stall.claimable_here`), but it does not end #6298's run-continuing containment early: today the drain waits, #6298 frees the root at the 600 s ceiling, holds the file and the run carries on, and firing at the 150 s allowance would turn that recovery into a `blocked` exit for every one-shot, cron and agent caller. So: from the allowance to the ceiling the drain keeps going and prints the existing live `stalled <N>s on <step>` line with the owner pid, kind and C3 triple every 10 s (and B4 flips `data_moving` at the ceiling); `drain_stalled` fires, regardless of `claimable_here`, only when the ceiling passes without the root being released, or when the owner's row is `wedged`/`restart_required`, naming request, phase, step, owner host, pid and nonce (the `claim_phase` stamp from #6298 has all of it; #6298 already dropped the lease columns from the fingerprint). The lapsed-owner case: `claimable_here` is the wrong predicate (`claimableWriteSql` requires `state='queued'`, `pr6298:journal.ts:470,503-506`, and a lapsed head is still `running`), so the drain waits one more window only when `head_lapsed` is true, the worktree's owner is this host and a live full row exists in `persistence_consumers`; otherwise it fires at once with `cause: owner_missing`. Gate item G5: fire at the allowance (the issue's literal ask) vs at the ceiling (this text, the default). The existing `preparation_abandoned` path (owner is this process) is unchanged. The stop's envelope changes with its meaning: a new `cause: owner_wedged_here` carries the owner pid and kind, `retry_after_ms` set to the time left until the 600 s ceiling (after which #6298 frees the root and the next pass holds the entry), `next.safe_to_loop: true` and `fix.next: wait` while the ceiling is ahead, and `fix.next: tell_user_to_run` (restart the named pid) once it has passed; the `why` text "and nothing here can claim it" (`pr6298:sync-drain.ts:536-541`), the `drain_stalled` row in `docs/guides/write-refusals.md:416` and the live-sync outcome table are rewritten to match. Window: the stall line starts at the allowance #6298 computes (`max(STALL_MS 30 s, budget 120 s + STALL_GRACE_MS 30 s)` after `STALL_PASSES` 3 passes, measured from the stamp's `step_since`); the exit waits for `persistence.preparation_ceiling_ms`, which stays untouched. Progress, for B2, B4 and I2 alike, is a committed receipt or a step advance (`step_since` moving): a 64-member group prepares in waves of four (`pr6298:group-publish.ts:553-575`) and commits nothing until every wave is done, so a per-member budget is not a group budget and only a head whose step has not advanced for the whole window is stalled.
- B3. `writer status --json` carries `host.consumers`: the processes on this host that hold a consumer (pid, kind from `claimOwner().kind` in `claim-phase.ts`, build, started_at, mode `full|waiter_only`, and the C1 pool numbers), from a small `persistence_consumers` heartbeat table (one row per process, primary key `(host_id, pid, nonce)` with `pid_ns` and `started_at` columns, so a reused pid after a container restart or across pid namespaces is a new row, renewed by its own single-flight 10 s timer in every full consumer, independent of claim renewal (an idle `serve` holds no claim), through `executeRawDirect` like the claim lease; that leaves the ordinary pool only when the dual pool is active (`pr6298:postgres-engine.ts:2804-2813`), so with a single URL a starved pool does stop the heartbeat, and that is acceptable: the row is a liveness signal, not a progress signal, and a starved owner losing suppression is the wanted outcome; the row also carries the owner's `host_json_path`, `persistence_home` and `minted_under` so I1 can name the owner's file without reading its filesystem, and the `wedged` fields of B1a; `live` = renewed within 30 s, `lapsed` = 60 s; each renewal statement also deletes rows lapsed for more than 60 s in the same CTE, idempotent, no lock; a failed heartbeat write is logged once and never stops the consumer). This is one new schema migration (number taken at the merge slot, `bun run new:migration`, PGLite and Postgres parity, `schema.sql` plus the golden tests).
