**Facts from your conversations land on you only when the page shows which speaker you are.**

### Changed
- Fact extraction from a conversation page now files a fact under the brain's owner only when the page proves which speaker the owner is: a `speaker_owner` entry its own importer wrote. For the owner's own statements, one of the owner's turns must also be in that part of the conversation. A claim someone else makes about the owner keeps the owner on such a page. On every other page (meeting transcripts, imported chat logs, a page an MCP caller wrote), a fact the model filed under the owner keeps no subject and carries the note `speaker unknown`. It stays on its page and in search, so nothing is hidden. This fixes meeting recordings that put the owner's name on other people's lines and filed their facts as the owner's.
- A name in the text no longer makes the owner a fact's subject, in conversation extraction and in the facts written alongside an ordinary page write.
- When the owner's email sits on several person pages (a network or cohort directory entry, a contact card), each of them now counts as the owner and nobody has to pick one: the owner's facts go to the owner member's page (`memory/members/<id>`, else `memory/owner`), an unproven fact filed on any of them is speaker-unknown, and none of those pages is merged or edited. `gbrain doctor`'s `owner_identity` check no longer warns about it, and `gbrain identity --json` lists those pages under `pages` instead of a `several_pages` conflict.
- On a page that proves the owner's speaker, the extractor is told which label is the owner and which page their facts belong on: the member page `memory/members/<id>` when the brain has an owner member, else `memory/owner`. The extraction system prompt is unchanged.
- A `speaker_owner` entry counts only on a source that belongs to one person: the one member it is bound to, or the owner on a brain no other person shares. Transcript imports and the conversation archive now use the same rule as the Gmail and iMessage connectors, so a source several people share gets no entry. Meetings sources are no longer trusted for the entry, and a write that skipped the journal counts only from the local CLI or the host application.
