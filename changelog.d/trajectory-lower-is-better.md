**`gbrain eval trajectory` and `gbrain founder scorecard` no longer call falling churn, burn or cost a regression; they flag the rise instead.**

### Fixed
- For lower-is-better metrics (the exact names `burn_rate`, `churn_rate`, `cac`, `burn`, `churn`, and any metric with the whole word cost, expense, loss, latency, payback or debt, plus costs, expenses, losses, debts), a rise of 10% or more is the regression ("burn rose 33.3%") and a drop is not. Every other metric keeps "a 10% drop is a regression". `lossless`, `costco` and `debtor` do not match. A name that also holds `saving`, `savings`, `saved`, `recovered`, `recovery`, `reduction`, `reduced`, `avoided` or `coverage` (`cost_savings`, `revenue_loss_recovered`, `debt_service_coverage_ratio`) is not lower-is-better: a rise is good news and a drop is the regression. When the older value of a lower-is-better metric is negative (a loss stored as -50000), the magnitudes are compared, so -50000 to -100000 is a +100% rise and -50000 to a profit of 100000 is an improvement. A change of exactly the threshold is flagged even when float rounding puts it a hair under (`0.05` to `0.055`). The whole list is one constant, `LOWER_IS_BETTER` in src/core/trajectory.ts, so a later change is one edit.
- Each `regressions[]` entry in `find_trajectory` and `eval trajectory --json` gains an optional `direction` (`down` or `up`); `schema_version` stays 1. The text marker reads `[REGRESSION ↑N%]` for a rise.
- The scorecard's `growth_trajectory` delta now divides by the absolute prior value, so a loss shrinking from -100000 to -50000 reads `up`, +50%, like the regression math.
