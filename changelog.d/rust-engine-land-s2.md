**The decide store, decide proposals, open loops, timeline reads and the write-side reads now sit behind engine members, and nothing changes for users.**

### Changed
- The queries behind the decide store and its proposals, open loops, timeline reads and the write-side reads (access tokens, memory proposals, source state, the health memo and similar) now live in `src/core/engine-sql/` and are called through seventy new `BrainEngine` members on PGLite, Postgres and the opt-in Rust engine. These code paths now call the members: `gbrain auth list`, the doctor timeline-history check, the open-loop store (upsert, close, list), the decide store, the decide proposals store and review lane, the health memo clock, notifier status, timeline repair, `whoknows`, and the source reads in sync preflight, the sync connector and write-through. PGLite and Postgres brains return the same results as before; the callers moved to the members and the SQL did not change.
- Postgres open-loop and memory-proposal writes (`gbrain loops done`, the loop upsert, proposal expiry and purge, and attaching a source path) still retry once when a pooled connection starts read-only, as they did before the move.
- On the Rust engine the decide store, decide proposals and open loops members work. Nine timeline-read members and twenty-one write-side members have no native Rust method yet, so they refuse with `engine_capability_missing` until one lands. The open-loops Rust adapter registers only part of the loop reads (`closedLoopWithActiveFact` and `loopFactDrift` are not registered). On the Rust engine the entity card now keeps only the open loops of the page's own source, and a page whose slug matches more than 1,000 open loops is refused instead of answered with a partial list.
- On Postgres, the doctor checks still stop at their time budget when a decide, timeline, notifier or worktree-binding read is stuck behind a lock, instead of running past it.
