On a source configured `canonical: git`, a write whose door commit landed in Git and whose database transaction then failed before committing (an owner exception after the commit) is now replayed by its owner instead of ending `failed` / `storage_error`: the replay finds its change already committed and records it without a second commit. A `forget` caught there now records its withdrawal, so the fact turns inactive and remembering the same claim is refused `fact_withdrawn`; before, the fact stayed active until a sync, no withdrawal was recorded (so the claim could be remembered again) and the reply said nothing was saved. A replay that fails as well ends failed with the commit named on its receipt (`landed_before_row`), and a `remember` or `forget` reply then names that commit instead of saying nothing was saved. `gbrain repair failed-writes` lists a refused `forget` as a caller write to resubmit instead of one the next dream cycle produces again.
