gbrain eval trajectory, gbrain founder scorecard and the find_trajectory tool now know that for some metrics lower is better. For burn_rate, churn_rate, cac, burn, churn and any metric with the whole word cost, expense, loss, latency, payback or debt (or costs, expenses, losses, debts) unless the name also has saving, savings, saved, recovered, recovery, reduction, reduced, avoided, coverage, ratio or ratios (so cost_savings, revenue_loss_recovered, debt_service_coverage_ratio and benefit_cost_ratio stay ordinary metrics), a drop is no longer a regression or red flag; a rise of 10% or more is, and the scorecard says "burn rose 33.3%". Every other metric still flags a 10% drop. A series of a lower-is-better metric that starts negative (a loss stored as -50000) compares magnitudes at every step, chosen once per series: -50000 to -100000 is a +100% rise, -50000 to a profit of 100000 is an improvement, and 100000 back to -50000 is a rise. A series that starts positive uses the plain change. A change of exactly the threshold is flagged even when float rounding puts it a hair under (0.05 to 0.055). Each regressions entry gains an optional direction field (down or up); schema_version stays 1. The scorecard's growth delta also divides by the absolute prior value, so a loss shrinking from -100000 to -50000 reads up.
