Shadow mode carries its coverage watermark from one short gbrain serve session to the next only when the primary shows no write in between. It now judges that from the database's own write counters for the copied tables and the statistics epoch, not from the writer records, which a restricted role, locked evidence tables or a refreshed older row could leave unchanged. A seal is refused while another connection has a write open, a prepared transaction exists, a recorded writer is still connected, another connection of a role that can write a copied table (itself, or through a role it can switch to with SET ROLE) is not idle, went idle less than 61 seconds ago or cannot be seen by the reading role (a read-only connection such as a monitoring agent is never waited on), or a login role can write without a way to record it, and on PostgreSQL older than 15 or with track_counts off. A connection the writer hooks recorded stays refused while it is connected, even after it commits (`other_writer_live`), until it disconnects. When that happens the next session runs a full reconcile pass, and status names the reason. A confirmation whose answer was lost is withdrawn (`seal_confirm_unacknowledged`), and a withdrawal is sent once at once and again after waits of 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 seconds; if none is acknowledged the close does not seal, and the next reconcile check withdraws it again and scans fresh.
